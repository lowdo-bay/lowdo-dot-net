Texas Architect Magazine feature by Aaron Seward profiling the Dakota Mountain house in Dripping Springs, Texas, designed by Low Design Office. The article examines how sustainable, affordable residential architecture can address contemporary challenges including housing shortage and climate change.

Key design features highlighted:
- Distinctive double-roof system with cedar timbers providing solar protection and outdoor living spaces
- Passive solar design reducing electricity use by 72 percent compared to regional averages
- 33,000-gallon rainwater collection system
- Cross-ventilation strategies for natural cooling
- Co-living design accommodating multiple generations with single-level accessibility
- Project cost of approximately $162 per square foot, competitive with production homebuilders

The residence demonstrates how thoughtful design can address pressing issues of equity, sustainability, and housing affordability while delivering high-quality architecture.
